IEEE 30-bus power system analysis

ELE 4343 Advanced Power Systems · MATLAB · Nov 2026 · team of 4

We modelled the IEEE 30-bus benchmark as a utility network with growing demand, limited reactive support and exposure to three-phase faults, then studied it end to end.

My partNetwork data and per-unit model, the single-line diagram, and the source-data check: a script that compares 640 values in 21 fields against the published MATPOWER case — every value matches. I wrote the network-model section of the report.
  • Load flow: Gauss-Seidel and Newton-Raphson both converge at normal and +15% demand; losses rise from 17.55 to 24.03 MW.
  • Constraints found: branch 1–2 reaches 155.5% of its rating at high demand; three branches overloaded; buses 11 and 13 above the voltage limit.
  • Faults: 24 balanced-fault cases at six locations; highest level 1,453 MVA at bus 2.
  • Stability: critical clearing time 0.0706 s (≈4.2 cycles), so a 0.10 s relay + breaker loses synchronism — the corrective package raises it to 0.108 s.
  • Fixes: redispatch, extra circuits and new voltage set points remove every violation (highest loading 95.9% at normal demand).

  • Y-bus
  • Newton-Raphson
  • Z-bus faults
  • Equal Area Criterion

Vehicle damage classification

EGN 3113 Artificial Intelligence · MATLAB Deep Network Designer · Oct 2026

Classifies a cropped photo of car damage as Dent, Paint Chip, Scratch or Scuffs, comparing a small CNN trained from scratch with ResNet-18 transfer learning on the same held-out test set.

  • Dataset: 727 real photo crops built from two openly licensed annotated sources (CC BY 4.0 and MIT), cropped with 8% context.
  • No leakage: exact-hash and perceptual grouping kept near-duplicate photos in the same split (70 / 15 / 15).
  • Result: ResNet-18 reached 49.5% test accuracy vs 31.2% for the scratch CNN — an 18.4-point gain.
  • Explainability: Grad-CAM maps show where each model looked, including on its mistakes.
  • Honest limits: neither model is reliable enough for unattended damage assessment; the report names the small scuff set and source bias as the reasons.

Task-aware PPE detection In progress

Capstone Design Project · proposal submitted Oct 2026 · industry partner in Dubai

In workshops, PPE compliance is checked by supervisors on walk-rounds. Existing detection models are not task-based, are usually tested in lab conditions, and struggle with small items like goggles. This project aims to check, for each worker and each required item, whether the right PPE is worn for the task at hand.

  • Objectives: benchmark current PPE-detection models, build an annotated dataset of the partner's required PPE items, and develop a per-worker, per-item compliance method.
  • Success target: mAP ≥ 0.85 and recall ≥ 0.90 in the real workshop.
  • Constraints: worker privacy, no disruption to site operations, data approval from the partner.
